# Student Interview Notes

## Interview 1: B.Tech CS, 3rd year, Tier-2 college
- Quote: "I can code the solution, but as soon as the interviewer asks why this is O(n), my mind goes blank."
- Insight: Candidate felt confident in syntax but froze while explaining time and space complexity aloud.
- Pain point: Silent IDEs encourage fast typing instead of verbal reasoning.

## Interview 2: ECE final-year student
- Quote: "In mock interviews, I often say 'uh' and then restart. It feels like I am not allowed to think out loud, and that destroys my confidence."
- Insight: Filler words and dead air were compounded by the fear of being judged for pauses.
- Pain point: Interview practice lacks a communication-aware feedback system.

## Interview 3: Data Structures student, placement prep cohort
- Quote: "I know the brute-force logic, but when they ask for the optimal approach, I jump straight to the answer without explaining the trade-off."
- Insight: Students often ignore the requirement to narrate the reasoning path.
- Pain point: There is no scaffolding to teach a structured verbal pitch before coding.

## Interview 4: CSE student, internship drive
- Quote: "I solve on LeetCode but then freeze on a live problem because the recruiter interrupts with an edge-case question."
- Insight: The candidate was comfortable with coding but not with real-time curveballs and live follow-up questions.
- Pain point: Standard coding platforms do not simulate interviewer interruptions.

## Interview 5: Mechanical engineering graduate aiming for software roles
- Quote: "When I speak in Hinglish, I know the logic is correct, but I cannot convert it into polished corporate English under time pressure."
- Insight: Bilingual students need a translation bridge and speaking support, not just raw transcription.
- Pain point: There is a gap between natural explanation and professional interview English.

## Cross-cutting pattern
Across all five interviews, students repeatedly described frustration with silent coding environments, dead air, and pressure to explain algorithmic reasoning while also solving code under time constraints. This validated the need for an active, voice-first interview simulator with communication guardrails.
